Why it matters

You have already seen a fake today

You just could not tell. These are the four ways it usually reaches people.

The forwarded photo

A shocking image lands in the family group at midnight. By morning it is on four more groups. Nobody checked — because checking felt impossible.

The profile that does not exist

A friendly face, a normal job, a few photos. The person was generated by a model minutes before the account was created.

The video call from "family"

Same face, same voice, urgent money request. Deepfake video calls have already cost people their savings.

The "verified" document

Fake IDs, fake matrimonial photos, fake job offers — all now come with a face that passes a casual glance.

The problem is not that people are careless. It is that until now, checking took an expert.

Straight answers

Questions people actually ask

Is it always right?

No, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. On our test set it is right 99.4% of the time, but images that have been screenshotted, heavily compressed or made by a brand-new generator are harder. That is why every result comes with an uncalibrated model score instead of a bare yes or no.

What kind of fakes does it catch best?

Fully AI-generated faces — the kind behind fake profiles. Face-swap videos are a different technique and are the next thing on our roadmap.

Do I need a powerful computer?

No. It was built and tested on an Intel i3 with 8 GB RAM, CPU only. A photo takes well under a second once the models are loaded.

Does my photo get uploaded anywhere?

No. Processing happens on the machine running DeepShield, and the file is deleted as soon as the analysis finishes.

Can I use the result as proof?

Treat it as a strong signal, not as forensic evidence. Use it to decide whether something deserves a closer look — and verify important claims through more than one source.
